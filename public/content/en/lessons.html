How is the course structured?

The course includes 24 lessons. You start with the home row, then each lesson adds two keys in order of their frequency in English: first the most common letters like e, i, r, u, t, o, which make up more than 40% of all letters in English text, and later the rare ones like x, q, z. This way you type real words from the earliest lessons, not just letter combinations. Each group of lessons ends with a review lesson.

After the letters come a lesson on capitals, punctuation marks, question and exclamation marks, apostrophes and quotes, hyphens, numbers, common words, sentences and famous quotes.

How is each lesson built?

  • Motion practice: Each new key is practiced together with the home row key it comes from, like jf for the j key. This teaches you both how to reach the key and how to return to the home row.
  • Real words: Most of the lesson consists of words that you can type with the keys you already know, with emphasis on the new keys. Fixed words gradually become one smooth motion.
  • Review what you learned: Each lesson includes words with keys from earlier lessons, so they do not fade.
  • Accuracy first: If you press the wrong key, the lesson waits until you press the correct one.

Blind mode

At first the virtual keyboard and hands show you which finger presses which key. As the keys become familiar, turn on blind mode: the keyboard on screen disappears and the hint appears only if you pause. This way the help fades gradually, and your fingers learn to find the keys on their own.

Stars

You get one star for finishing a lesson, two stars for 92% accuracy or better, and three stars for 97% accuracy or better and reaching the lesson's target speed. It is a good idea to repeat a lesson until you get three stars before moving on.

Mistakes in real typing lessons

In the letter lessons the lesson waits for you to press the correct key. In the four real typing lessons (common words, sentences and quotes) you continue typing even after a mistake and correct it with Backspace, just like in normal typing. This way you practice noticing and fixing your own mistakes.

Why do I type slower at first?

If you already type with two fingers, you will type slower in the first weeks. This is expected and normal. In a study by Hebrew University on a touch typing course, students typed slower at the end of the course than at the start, but three months after the course they typed faster than they did before. The biggest risk is going back to peeking at the keyboard because it feels faster, so it is important to keep your eyes on the screen.
