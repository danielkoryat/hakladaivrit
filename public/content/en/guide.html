What is touch typing?

Touch typing, also called ten-finger typing, is typing without looking at the keyboard. Each finger controls a fixed set of keys, and your fingers always return to the same starting point: the home row. With practice, your brain remembers the key positions (muscle memory) and your eyes stay on the screen.

Someone who types with two fingers and searches visually for keys usually reaches 20-30 words per minute. With touch typing you can reach 50, 60 words per minute or more, with far fewer mistakes and less fatigue.

Home row on the QWERTY keyboard

The home row is the middle row of keys. This is where your fingers rest when you are not typing, and where they return after each keystroke:

  • Left hand: pinky on a, ring on s, middle on d, index on f.
  • Right hand: index on j, middle on k, ring on l, pinky on ;.
  • Thumbs: on the space bar.

The keys f and j have a small bump on them. Use this bump to find the home row by touch, without looking.

Which finger presses each key?

Each finger controls a "column" of keys: the home row key, the key above, the key below, and the number at the top. The index fingers control two columns each.

For capital letters, hold Shift with the opposite hand: a left-hand letter with the right Shift, and a right-hand letter with the left Shift. Numbers are typed with the same finger that types the letter above them.

Correct posture at the keyboard

  • Sit upright, with your feet flat on the floor and your screen at eye level.
  • Keep your elbows close to your body at roughly a 90-degree angle.
  • Keep your wrists straight and floating above the keyboard, not resting on it or bent.
  • Keep your fingers slightly curved and touching the keys only with their tips.
  • Press gently. There is no need to press all the way down.

Recommended learning plan

  1. Days 1-2: Home row. Lessons 1-4. Focus on accuracy, not speed.
  2. Days 3-5: Common letters. Lessons 5-8: e, i, r, u, t, o. The most frequent letters in English. Return to the home row after every keystroke.
  3. Week 2: More letters. Lessons 9-15. By the end of the week you will know all 26 letters.
  4. Week 3: Capitals and punctuation. Lessons 16-21, and run a typing test each day.
  5. Week 4: Real typing. Lessons 22-24: common words, sentences and quotes.
  6. Beyond that: 10-15 minutes per day of smart practice on your weak keys, and texts from the library.

It is better to practice 15 minutes every day than two hours once a week. In a classic study of typing learning, students who practiced one hour per day learned faster than those who practiced two hours a day. Muscle memory builds from repeated practice and rest in between.

In the first weeks you will probably type slower than your old way. This is a natural transition phase, and in studies speed rose above the previous level after a few months of use. Do not peek at the keyboard when it feels hard: it feels faster now, but it stops your learning.

Common mistakes

  • Looking at the keyboard. Peeking feels faster, but it prevents your brain from learning. It is better to type slowly and make mistakes. When keys become familiar, turn on blind mode in the lessons, which even hides the keyboard shown on screen.
  • Switching fingers. Fast typists always press each letter with the same finger. If you sometimes press a key with one finger and sometimes with another, your brain cannot build a habit.
  • Chasing speed too early. First aim for 95% accuracy or higher, and only then speed.
  • Not returning to the home row. After every keystroke your finger goes back to its home position, otherwise fingers start to drift.
  • Using the wrong finger for middle keys. The index fingers reach far in both directions to handle all the keys in the middle of the keyboard.
  • Neglecting your weak fingers. The pinky and ring fingers are weak at first, and because of that it matters most to practice them.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to learn touch typing?

With 15-20 minutes of practice per day, most people learn all the keys within a week or two, and reach their old typing speed within three to four weeks. Speed continues to improve after that.

Is touch typing free to learn?

Yes. This site has 24 progressive lessons, a typing test and smart practice, all free without signup required.

Do I need a keyboard with letter labels?

No. In touch typing you do not look at the keyboard anyway. The site detects the physical position of each key, so you can practice even if your computer is set to English.

Which finger should I use for the letter A?

Your left pinky. A is the home row key for the left pinky, and the keys Q and 1 belong to this finger too.

Which finger for letters in the middle, like E?

E is typed with the left middle finger, reaching up from D. The keys R and 3 also belong to the left middle finger.

Is English touch typing different from other languages?

The method is the same: the same fingers for the same physical positions on the keyboard. Only the letters on the keys are different.
